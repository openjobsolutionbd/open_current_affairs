# Self-Reliance & Social Security

A country's self-reliance is the ability of its people to meet basic needs, use domestic resources efficiently and drive socio-economic development with minimal dependence on foreign aid or imports. Social security is a government-run system that protects citizens from financial hardship by providing income support and essential services during events such as old age, unemployment, illness, disability, or other life risks.

## Self-Reliance

The self-reliance of a country's people refers to their capacity to independently meet basic needs, sustain livelihoods, and drive local socio-economic growth. It involves reducing dependency on external aid and imports by leveraging local skills, agricultural resources, and community collaboration. Self-reliance, popularized by Ralph Waldo Emerson in his 1841 essay *Self-Reliance*, emphasizes self-confidence, independent thinking, creativity, and reliance on one's own abilities. Mahatma Gandhi later linked it to simple living and sustainable consumption. In Bangladesh, self-reliance focuses on food security, local production, entrepreneurship, and efficient use of domestic resources to reduce dependence on foreign aid and imports.

## Social Security

Social Security for a country's population refers to public measures, such as cash transfers, pensions, and unemployment insurance, designed to protect individuals and families against life-cycle risks like disability, old age, unemployment and poverty. The modern concept of social security was popularized by Franklin D. Roosevelt through the Social Security Act of 1935. Social security is a government system of policies, programs and public measures designed to protect individuals and families from economic and social risks throughout their lives. It provides income security, healthcare, and financial assistance during situations such as:
- old age
- disability
- maternity
- poverty
- students with disabilities
- unemployment
- illness
- workplace injury
- loss of income

Financed mainly through taxes and public contributions, it acts as an economic safety net, ensuring a minimum standard of living, social justice, human dignity, and social inclusion while reducing poverty, inequality, and vulnerability and promoting sustainable socio-economic development.

## Key Features of Social Security

Social security is a comprehensive system of public policies and programmes designed to protect individuals and families from economic and social risks throughout their lives. By reducing poverty, promoting social justice, and protecting vulnerable groups, social security contributes to inclusive development, economic stability, and overall social well-being.

- **Income Security:** Provides financial assistance during old age, sickness, unemployment, disability, maternity, work-related injuries, and the loss of a family breadwinner, ensuring basic income stability.
- **Comprehensive Coverage:** Protects the entire population or targeted vulnerable groups, including low-income families, the elderly, women, children, persons with disabilities, unemployed workers and marginalized communities.
- **Social Protection and Poverty Reduction:** Reduces poverty, income inequality, and economic vulnerability through cash transfers, food assistance, subsidies and other welfare programmes.
- **Access to Essential Services:** Ensures access to healthcare, pensions, education support, maternity care, disability services, housing assistance, nutrition programmes, and other basic social services.
- **Risk Management:** Protects individuals and households from financial shocks caused by illness, accidents, natural disasters, unemployment, or other unforeseen crises, helping them recover more quickly.

## Social Security in Budget

The Social Security Budget allocates Tk 1,44,338 crore across 90 programmes in FY2026-27, the largest in Bangladesh's history and 14% higher than the previous year. The flagship Family Card Programme will provide Tk 2,500 per month through digital transfers to 41 lakh poor households using Proxy Means Testing (PMT), while the Farmer's Card Programme offers an annual grant of Tk 2,500 to eligible farmers. Although the allocation increased spending on public employment, education, food distribution, disaster assistance, and school stipends has declined. Moreover, most of the social protection budget is concentrated on household poverty and elderly programmes. The budget also expands welfare by increasing Old Age Allowance and Disability Allowance. Overall, the budget strengthens social protection but requires more balanced, transparent, and human capital-oriented investment to achieve a self-reliant and inclusive Bangladesh.

## Priority Challenges for Social Security

Despite remarkable progress, Bangladesh's National Social Security System continues to face several structural and implementation challenges. These include limited coverage of poor and informal-sector workers, inaccurate beneficiary targeting, resource constraints, overlapping programmes, inadequate investment in early childhood, education, health and employment, and an overconcentration of spending on pensions and cash transfers. Strengthening transparency, digital governance, monitoring, and service delivery, while ensuring efficient resource allocation and a life-cycle-based, inclusive social protection system, is essential to reduce poverty and inequality, enhance resilience to economic and climate shocks, and build a self-reliant, equitable, and sustainable Bangladesh.

## Vision for Social Security

The Government of Bangladesh has a constitutional responsibility to ensure social security for its citizens. The long-term vision of the National Social Security Strategy (NSSS) is to build an inclusive, rights-based, and comprehensive social security system that guarantees minimum income and protects people from poverty, economic shocks, and life-cycle risks. It also aims to reduce poverty and inequality while promoting human development, employment, and sustainable economic growth. To achieve this vision, the government seeks to improve the efficiency of resource allocation, strengthen service delivery, and expand social protection, with priority given to the poorest and most vulnerable groups.

Self-reliance and social security are complementary pillars of sustainable development. While self-reliance promotes productivity, innovation, entrepreneurship, and efficient use of domestic resources, social security protects vulnerable people from poverty and life-cycle risks. By investing in education, skills, employment, healthcare, digital governance, and targeted social protection, Bangladesh can build a self-reliant, resilient, inclusive, and prosperous society where every citizen has the opportunity to contribute to and benefit from national development.
